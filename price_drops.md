# PC Part Price Drops — July 15, 2026

> **Market Context:** GPU, RAM, and SSD prices remain elevated across the board in mid-2026 due to AI data center demand consuming the majority of global memory output (IDC forecasts AI could absorb up to 70% of world memory production in 2026, up from ~25% in 2022). NAND contract prices are up ~246% year-over-year. Despite this, promotional sales events — including Newegg's 4th of July Sale, Amazon Prime Day, and Best Buy's Techtober — are producing real discounts worth tracking.

- **NVIDIA Blackwell (RTX 50-series)** is fully launched, with the RTX 5060, 5060 Ti, 5070, 5070 Ti, and 5080/5090 all available. However, the RTX 5090 has seen retail listings spike to **$4,300–$5,000**, roughly 50%+ over MSRP, driven by memory chip shortages.

- **Intel Arc B580/B570** remain the best value budget options and are in good supply.
- At CES 2026, NVIDIA unveiled its next-gen **Vera Rubin** AI platform and AMD announced its **Helios** rack-scale platform, both targeting 2026 production — signaling continued supply pressure for consumer DRAM/memory as AI accelerators take priority.

## RAM (Memory)

### Technology News
- **DDR5 prices have spiked dramatically.** A 32GB DDR5-6000 kit that cost $80–$120 a year ago now starts around **$390–$400** for brand-name options. AI memory demand is the primary driver.
- **DDR4 is being phased out** but still viable for older platforms at lower prices. For new builds on Intel Arrow Lake or AMD Ryzen 9000, DDR5 is required.
- Prices eased ~2.2% over the past week per WhereIsMyRam tracking — the trend is modest but suggests retailers are testing lower price points ahead of Prime Day.
- **Bundle deals** (RAM + motherboard combos) are currently offering the best effective value, often saving $100–$220 compared to buying standalone.
- Newegg's 4th of July sale included coupon codes providing significant cuts on Corsair, G.Skill, and Kingston kits — some codes may still be active.

## SSDs (Solid State Drives)

### Technology News
- **NAND prices are up 246% YoY** — 1TB Gen 4 drives that were $75 in 2025 now start around **$159**. Tariffs and geopolitical supply chain issues are compounding the shortage.
- **PCIe Gen 5 SSDs** (e.g., Crucial T710, upcoming WD Gen 5 drives) are now more accessible at the high end, offering read speeds exceeding 12,000 MB/s, but remain pricey.
- **Samsung 990 EVO Plus** launched with a PCIe Gen 4×4 / Gen 5×2 hybrid interface (up to 7,250 MB/s reads) and has become a popular mid-range pick.
- Analysts forecast no meaningful NAND price relief before Q4 2026, so current promotional prices represent genuine windows of opportunity.
- **M.2 2230 SSDs** for handheld gaming (Steam Deck, ASUS ROG Ally) are in high demand, with the WD SN770M being a top pick for upgrades.
