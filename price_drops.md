## RAM (Memory)

### Market News
The RAM market is in a significant pricing crisis in 2026. DDR5 prices have risen **3–5x** since October 2025, driven by NAND and DRAM shortages as AI/data center demand consumes manufacturing capacity. Kingston confirmed a **246% increase in NAND wafer prices**. Gartner forecasts a +130% price increase by end of 2026, with no real normalization before Q4 2027. A 32 GB DDR5-6000 kit that was ~$80–100 in mid-2024 now retails for **$300–$470**. Despite the crunch, Newegg's 4th of July sale featured notable DDR5 coupon deals (many now expired), and combo/bundle deals remain the best way to save. Buy now if you need it — prices are unlikely to fall meaningfully through 2026.

## SSDs (Solid State Drives)

### Market News
SSD prices have climbed sharply through 2026 as AI workloads absorb NAND manufacturing capacity — all major manufacturers report 2026 capacity is effectively allocated to data centers. NVMe Gen 4 pricing now sits at **$60–80/TB** (up from $30–40/TB in 2023). PCIe Gen 5 drives like the WD Black SN8100 are hitting sequential reads of **14,900 MB/s**, but cost $90–150/TB. On the enterprise frontier, Samsung is targeting **256 TB PCIe 6.0** SSDs in 2026 and 512 TB by 2027. For consumers, 4 TB Gen 4 drives currently offer the best $/TB ratio (~$70–90/TB), with the Kingston NV3 remaining the cheapest budget option (~$60/TB). Meaningful price relief is not expected before late 2027.
