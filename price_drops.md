## Graphics Cards (GPUs)

### Market News

The GPU market in mid-2026 is under supply pressure. NVIDIA has cut RTX 50-series production by 20–40% year-over-year due to HBM memory constraints tied to AI data-center demand. High-end cards (RTX 5080, 5090) remain well above MSRP with poor availability. The RTX 5080 costs ~39% more than the RTX 5070 Ti while delivering only ~17% more performance, making the 5070 Ti and AMD alternatives the better value picks right now. AMD's RX 9070 and RX 9070 XT offer competitive raster performance at or near NVIDIA equivalents' prices, and Newegg's FantasTech/4th of July sale brought several near-MSRP deals. NVIDIA DLSS 4 Multi-Frame Generation continues to widen the frame-rate gap for NVIDIA owners in supported titles, while AMD FSR 4 has improved significantly on RDNA 4 hardware.

## RAM (Memory)

### Market News

DDR5 RAM is in a full-blown supply crisis. Prices are up 300–400% compared to pre-shortage (early 2025) levels, driven by AI data-center infrastructure consuming DRAM production capacity. A 32GB (2×16GB) DDR5-6000 CL30 kit that cost ~$90 in 2024 now runs $420–$460 at normal retail. Analysts expect prices to remain elevated or rise further through the rest of 2026 and into 2027. Newegg's FantasTech/4th of July sale featured time-limited coupon codes (JSF543, JSF545, JSF563) that cut prices meaningfully. Notably, some G.Skill and Corsair DDR5 listings on Newegg briefly appeared at $4,000 placeholder prices — a sign of speculative price-floor testing during the shortage. DDR4 remains stable and affordable — a valid choice for budget builds on compatible platforms (AM4, Intel 12th/13th/14th Gen).

## SSDs (Solid State Drives)

### Market News

NAND flash prices surged approximately 5× by January 2026, driven by AI infrastructure demand. Finding a 2TB NVMe SSD under $140 is now difficult; 4TB drives typically run $250–$500+. Crucial discontinued the T500 in February 2026, causing remaining stock to spike 68% in a single week. However, Amazon Prime Day and Newegg's FantasTech sale triggered aggressive clearance pricing from Samsung and WD, creating some of the best deals since the price run-up began. Samsung's 990 Pro 2TB entered final clearance at ~42–44% off as Samsung shifts focus to next-gen successors. The WD Black SN7100 (PCIe Gen 4, 7,250 MB/s read / 6,900 MB/s write) is the current benchmark for gaming-focused NVMe. PCIe Gen 5 SSDs remain expensive and largely overkill for gaming, so Gen 4 drives offer the best price-to-performance ratio in 2026.
