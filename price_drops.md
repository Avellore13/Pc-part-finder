## 💾 RAM (Memory)

### Tech News
- **DDR6 still years away:** All major DRAM makers (Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron) have DDR6 in development, but commercialization is not expected until the early 2030s. DDR6 spec targets up to 17,600 MT/s and ~134 GB/s bandwidth.
- **2026 DRAM shortage ongoing:** AI infrastructure demand has consumed DRAM supply, pushing DDR5 32GB kit prices from ~$80 (summer 2025) to $379–$500+ today — a 485%+ increase for some kits. Prices are not expected to drop meaningfully until 2027.
- **LPCAMM2 gaining traction:** Framework's Laptop 13 Pro now supports LPCAMM2 modules, signaling a slow-moving but real shift away from SO-DIMM in thin laptops. Desktop DIMM form factors are unaffected.
- **HBM3E ramp-up:** Micron is targeting ~100,000 HBM wafers/month by end of 2026 — primarily for AI accelerators, not consumer desktops.

## 💿 SSDs (Solid State Drives)

### Tech News
- **PCIe 5.0 goes mainstream in 2026:** The Samsung 9100 PRO (up to 14,800 MB/s read), WD Black SN8100, and SK Hynix Platinum P51 (14,700 MB/s) are leading the PCIe 5.0 consumer segment. Prices have come down enough that PCIe 5.0 is now a viable mainstream upgrade.
- **NAND supply crunch:** Kioxia/SanDisk announced a multi-year fab expansion through 2032, but near-term supply remains tight. NAND contract prices grew ~10–15% QoQ in Q3 2026. Consumer SSD prices are not expected to reliably drop until late 2026 or Q1 2027.
- **Samsung eyes PCIe 6.0 SSDs in enterprise:** Samsung is targeting 256 TB enterprise PCIe 6.0 SSDs in 2026, with 512 TB capacity in 2027. Consumer PCIe 6.0 is still 2–3 years away.
- **Gen 4 remains best $/TB value:** PCIe 4.0 drives average ~$76/TB in September 2026, the best price/performance tier for most consumer builds.
