### RAM Tech News
- **DDR6 — not yet at retail:** JEDEC finalized the LPDDR6 standard in July 2025 but the desktop DDR6 spec is still being ratified. Consumer DDR6 products are now expected in 2028–2029, not 2027 as earlier forecasts suggested.
- **DDR6 specs:** Starting speeds around 8,800 MT/s, scaling to 17,600 MT/s — roughly double DDR5 peak bandwidth. Supports 128Gb dies (enabling 64GB single-stick modules).
- **DDR6 pricing (projected):** A 32GB CAMM2/DDR6 module estimated at ~$500 at launch — roughly 5× the price of an equivalent DDR5 module today.
- **AI boom impact:** Enterprise DDR5 demand from AI training clusters is the primary driver of the current memory price crunch; retail prices are not expected to meaningfully drop until late 2026 or early 2027 at earliest.

### SSD Tech News
- **PCIe Gen 5 going mainstream:** Top Gen 5 drives now hit 14.8 GB/s sequential read and 2.3M+ IOPS. Gen 5 prices are falling on 2TB+ capacities, making them viable for enthusiast builds.
- **QLC NAND rising:** QLC flash now delivers ~90% of TLC performance while offering higher density at lower cost per TB. Rapidly becoming the preferred medium for AI-era, read-heavy storage workloads.
- **Price per GB:** 4TB Gen 4 SSDs are now dipping below $0.10/GB on deal — e.g., Crucial P3 at ~$0.05/GB is a standout value for bulk storage.
- **Samsung 990 EVO Plus 4TB** hit an all-time low recently with its PCIe Gen 5×2 / Gen 4×4 hybrid interface offering a middle ground between Gen 4 and full Gen 5 speeds.
- **NAND shortage:** Contract prices are expected to stabilize in Q3–Q4 2026 as new NAND fab capacity comes online, with possible relief for consumer prices by early 2027.
