## RAM (Memory)

### Market News
- **"RAMpocalypse" in full effect:** DDR5 prices surged 300–400% since Q3 2025 due to AI-driven DRAM shortages. A 32GB DDR5-6000 kit that cost $110–140 in Q3 2025 now runs $392–650.
- **Root cause:** Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron have shifted capacity toward High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) for AI datacenters, which consumes ~3× the wafer area per GB vs. standard DRAM. HBM commands far higher margins.
- **No near-term relief:** Analysts point to 2027 at the earliest for new fab capacity to reach volume. Global DRAM revenue surged 80% QoQ in Q1 2026.
- **Current pricing benchmark:** DDR5 is averaging ~$16.25/GB; DDR4 ~$7.47/GB as of August 23, 2026 (83rd percentile of the 5-week range — prices are above recent averages).

## SSDs (Solid State Drives)

### Market News
- **NAND flash prices surging:** AI datacenter demand has absorbed NAND production while manufacturers shifted capacity toward enterprise/QLC products. A 2TB NVMe drive that cost ~$120 a year ago now runs $215+.
- **4TB SSDs** that once hit $250 are now commonly priced above $500.
- **PCIe 5.0 SSDs** are emerging (Samsung 9100 PRO, Crucial P510) but carry a price premium from faster controllers and heatsink requirements. For gaming and everyday use, PCIe 4.0 offers nearly identical real-world performance at lower cost.
- **Crucial consumer line being wound down** — remaining first-party Crucial P310/P510 stock may not be replenished, making current pricing potentially the floor.
- **Relief timeline:** Analysts broadly point to 2027 when new fab capacity reaches volume.
