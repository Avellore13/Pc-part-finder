## 🧠 RAM (Memory)

### Technology News
- **DDR5 crisis deepens:** The 2026 RAM shortage is structural — DRAM manufacturers (Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron) have shifted wafer capacity from commodity DDR5 to HBM3/HBM3e for AI GPUs. 32GB DDR5 kits that sold near $100 in late 2024 now list around $370–$470. A 64GB DDR5 kit jumped 485% to ~$1,118 in August 2026.
- **No relief until 2027–2028:** Intel, Silicon Motion, and market analysts broadly expect the DRAM shortage to persist until 2027–2028 when new fab capacity (Samsung Taylor TX, TSMC N2 DRAM) comes online at volume.
- **DDR4 still viable:** DDR4 prices have risen too (averaging ~$7.47/GB vs the pre-shortage ~$2–3/GB), but remain significantly cheaper than DDR5. If you're on a DDR4 platform, now is a reasonable time to buy rather than wait.
- **Price trend:** DDR5 spot prices quadrupled since September 2025. Buy now if you need RAM — prices are expected to keep climbing through 2026 into 2027.

## 💾 SSDs (Solid State Drives)

### Technology News
- **NAND flash prices doubled:** NAND prices have roughly doubled since mid-2025, driven by the same AI data center dynamic — Samsung and SK Hynix cut consumer NAND output to produce higher-margin enterprise SSDs and 3D NAND for AI servers.
- **PCIe Gen 5 shortages:** Gen5 SSD controller shortages (driven by AI workload demand) have made high-end Gen5 drives scarce and expensive. The Samsung 9100 Pro (Gen5) lists around $478 for 1TB, vs the 990 Pro (Gen4) at ~$390 for 2TB.
- **Best value tier is Gen4:** PCIe Gen 4 NVMe drives currently offer the best price-per-TB ratio. The cheapest tracked Gen4 drives hit $105/TB as of late August 2026, vs Gen5 averaging significantly higher.
